# Language Revision: Martin + Gygax Voice

## Current voice

The manuscript is clear and consistent but **neutral and modern**: short declarative sentences, "Used by X" and "Referee may rule" without weight or colour. Little sensory detail, no archaic or period diction, and no Gygaxian formality.

**Example (current):**  
*"Pot, earthenware. Medieval use: The most common cooking vessel in peasant and urban households. Used for boiling pottage (grain-and-vegetable stew), porridge, and legumes."*

---

## Target voice: two strands

### 1. Martin strand (Medieval use / Materials)

- **Concrete and sensory:** rust, smoke, cold, grease, the heft of iron, the smell of tallow. Who held it, where it was used, what it cost in blood or coin.
- **Period rhythm and weight:** longer sentences where they add atmosphere; spare but vivid. No modern casualism ("pretty much", "a bit").
- **Stakes and consequence:** tools break, leather rots, copper turns green; the world is hard and things have history.
- **Diction:** occasional period-appropriate terms; "smallfolk", "serjeant", "pottage", "verdigris"; avoid anachronisms.

**Example (Martin-style):**  
*"Pot, earthenware. Medieval use: In peasant crofts and town kitchens alike, the earthen pot hung over the fire—pottage and porridge, beans and barley. Glaze held the broth; without it, the clay drank the liquor and left a taste of earth. Set in the coals or on a trivet; drop it, and the crack could spell a cold supper and a lost vessel."*

### 2. Gygax strand (Rules / Part I)

- **Authoritative and referee-centric:** "the referee shall", "the Dungeon Master may rule", "it is recommended that". The voice of the game master, not a textbook.
- **Precise mechanics unchanged:** all dice notation (1d6, 1d4), times (1 turn, 1 round), bonuses (+1, -2), and references to *Rules Cyclopedia* stay exact.
- **Formal tone:** "wherein", "thus", "hence", "therein"; avoid chatty or vague phrasing in rules.
- **Options stated clearly:** "at the referee's option", "the referee shall determine", "as the referee deems fit".

**Example (Gygax-style):**  
*"Rules: Suitable for boiling and stewing. If heated empty or over a fierce flame, or if dropped, the referee shall roll 1d6: on a result of 1, the pot cracks and is ruined. Unglazed pots absorb flavours; therein, contents may taint if used for different foods without washing."*

## Scope and approach

- **File:** [Peters-Extensive-List-of-Adventurer-Equipment.md](j:\Shared drives\Edens Efterklang\Regelbøger\Peters-Extensive-List-of-Adventurer-Equipment.md) (~2,298 lines).
- **Volume:** Foreword + Part I (~~130 lines); 42 section intros (~~80 lines); ~285 item blocks (each with Medieval use, Materials, Rules). Total prose to touch: roughly 1,000+ paragraphs.

**Recommended: phased pass**

1. **Phase 1 – Template:** Revise Foreword, Part I (Sections 1–6), and Part II intro. Revise **two full sections** (e.g. 7 Cooking Gear, 19 Traps or 20 Light and Fire) so every element (intro, Medieval use, Materials, Rules) matches the style guide. Use these as the reference for the rest.
2. **Phase 2 – Part II rollout:** Apply the same voice to the remaining 40 sections: section intro + every item’s Medieval use, Materials, and Rules. Work section-by-section to keep tone consistent.
3. **Phase 3 – Pass 2 (optional):** Light pass on Quick Reference and any remaining neutral phrases; ensure no "referee may" without "the" where Gygax would use "the referee"; trim any leftover modernisms.

**Alternative:** Single full pass from top to bottom (Foreword through Section 42), applying the same rules throughout. Same style guide; more risk of drift without the template step.

---

## Constraints

- **Do not change:** Table data (Cost, Enc, item names); section numbers; *Rules Cyclopedia* references; dice notation and mechanical outcomes (e.g. "1d6: on 1").
- **Do not add:** New rules, new items, or new mechanics.
- **Preserve:** All three-part structure (Medieval use / Materials / Rules); Danish project context (Edens Efterklang) is irrelevant—the book is in English and remains so.
